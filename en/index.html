Low-code is quick to pick up. The hard part is everything else.

Low-code platforms make development accessible. In a regulated environment, the difficulty is not the code: you have to understand the process, define a specification and stick to it, apply the standards in force, decide what must be traced, control access, anticipate validation and organize governance, so that the result stands up to audit.

A method proven in two worlds

Applied in medical diagnostic devices (bioMérieux) and in energy (Engie).

  1. Understand the process and the people who keep it running.
  2. Design a clear architecture.
  3. Simplify: tools that non-experts use without errors, with control by exception.
  4. Ensure compliance: risk analysis, validation package, test execution, GAMP 5, ALCOA+ data integrity, ISO 13485 and 21 CFR Part 11 compliance for the life sciences industry. In energy: simulation and statistical validation before announcing a gain.
  5. Deploy: documentation, user training, support, multi-site and international rollout.
  6. Improve: feedback from the field, controlled changes.

Projects

A custom MES (Manufacturing Execution System), connected to an ERP, for the assembly of medical diagnostic instruments

I designed and deployed an MES on a low-code platform, connected to SAP, and supervised its integration on the production lines. It orchestrates the system’s other applications: it distributes manufacturing orders to the assembly benches according to a DDMRP schedule, controls access to batch records, reports production times and tracks serial numbers through to finished products. About 116 h/year of paper-based management and traceability eliminated.

  • Low-code (Tulip)
  • SAP S/4HANA
  • ISA-95

Interconnected electronic batch records

Around the MES, I developed eleven electronic batch records, a semi-automated quality control system and control panels, which replace paper and update other departments in real time, including the warehouse: release by exception. Instrument data is fed back through a SCADA layer on Node-RED, in compliance with data integrity requirements (ALCOA+).
